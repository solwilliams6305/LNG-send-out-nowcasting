\title{Nowcasting European LNG send-out from free public data:\\
measured revision processes, calibrated filtering,\\ and the absence of a daily trading edge}
\author{Solomon J.\ Williams\\ \small University of Edinburgh}

\begin{abstract}
Daily gas send-out from north-west European LNG terminals is a direct
short-term driver of the TTF and NBP markets, and commercial vendors sell
reconstructions of it from proprietary satellite ship-tracking. We rebuild
the signal entirely from free public sources and add the two layers vendors
do not publish: a measurement of how the official data itself is revised,
and calibrated uncertainty validated against later-published truth. A
semi-Markov, Rao-Blackwellized particle filter delivers evening nowcasts
3--7$\times$ more accurate than any daily baseline, with distribution-free
coverage guarantees, and generalizes through the 2022--23 energy crisis.
En route we document several unpublished properties of the official data:
the platform's intraday ``estimate'' appears to be a carry-forward of the
previous day's value; published nominations forecast worse than
persistence; one gas day's official record permanently contradicts itself
by a full cargo. Intraday deviations follow a near-degenerate fractional
bridge ($\hat H\approx0.98$): each day is essentially one random ramp.
Cargo arrivals extracted from tank mass balance match vessel-class physics
to within one percent and are emphatically non-Poisson. Finally, we
backtest the signal set against front-month TTF under a pre-registered
protocol with costs, roll hygiene, and selection accounting, and report a
comprehensive negative: across 6.5 years, no daily-frequency directional
edge exists in any of these physical signals --- including supply-break
events, whose apparent edge inverts once crisis years are included,
because at the daily horizon causality runs from price to flows as much as
the reverse. The residual economic value of physical nowcasting
concentrates at sub-daily horizons and in calibrated uncertainty, which is
precisely where professional market access begins.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The quantity of liquefied natural gas regasified and sent into the
north-west European grid each day --- ``send-out'' --- is one of the few
physical variables that moves the continent's benchmark gas prices at
short horizons. It is also, nominally, public: transmission operators,
terminal operators, and the European transparency platforms all publish
versions of it. In practice the publications are slow, mutually
inconsistent, revised after the fact, and scattered across five
differently-shaped interfaces, which is why a commercial industry (Kpler,
Vortexa, ICIS) sells faster reconstructions built on proprietary satellite
ship-tracking.

This paper asks what can be recovered from the free layer alone, and
deliberately measures the parts the commercial products do not publish.
Our contributions:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{An open reconstruction with measured plumbing.} We fuse five
free sources (Section~\ref{sec:data}) and document their true timing and
failure modes, several of which contradict the platforms' own
documentation: publication leads of ten hours between sources measuring
the same quantity, hourly feeds trailing real time by two hours, silent
all-null series, and API queries that hang indefinitely.
\item \textbf{The revision processes of the official record}
(Section~\ref{sec:revisions}). Using retroactive version histories where
they exist and a thrice-daily snapshot archive where they do not, we
quantify how ``estimated'' becomes ``confirmed'': the United Kingdom's
three-rung maturity ladder collapses to two informative rungs; the
final-reconciliation rung never differs from its predecessor across 2{,}829
matched days; corrections have a heavy tail reaching 662.7\,GWh; and one
gas day's official record permanently contradicts itself by a full cargo.
\item \textbf{A calibrated nowcaster} (Sections~\ref{sec:model}--\ref{sec:eval}).
A semi-Markov switching model with explicit-duration regimes, marginalized
(Rao-Blackwellized) over its conditionally linear-Gaussian states, driven
by a renewal arrival process calibrated on 1{,}446 extracted cargo
discharges, observed through a fractional-bridge intraday channel, and
wrapped in adaptive conformal calibration. Evening nowcasts achieve
3--7$\times$ the accuracy of persistence with bands that empirically cover
at their nominal rates, evaluated walk-forward over 1{,}552 days per
terminal including the 2022--23 crisis.
\item \textbf{A comprehensive, disciplined negative}
(Section~\ref{sec:econ}). Under a pre-registered backtest protocol ---
information-aligned execution, transaction costs, roll hygiene,
Newey--West inference, explicit selection accounting, and a frozen 2026
holdout --- no daily-frequency directional edge on front-month TTF
survives in any of the signals, their changes, or their tails. We bound
the price response of ordinary flow surprises, show the market's standard
stress dial (storage fullness) is itself a poor directional signal, and
explain the sign inversion of supply-break trading by reverse causality at
crisis peaks.
\end{enumerate}

Three literatures intersect here without quite covering it. Vessel-arrival
prediction is dominated by statistical and machine-learning taxonomies
with no stochastic-process formulations \cite{eta_review}; the maritime
inventory-routing literature models exactly our constraint set but
prescriptively, with import-terminal inventories under-modelled and
uncertainty handling described as in its infancy \cite{mirp}; and the LNG
economics literature establishes that inter-regional arbitrage is
structurally damped \cite{arbitrage2014,realoption2020}, which our
market-response measurements corroborate from the physical side.

\section{Data: five free sources and their measured behaviour}
\label{sec:data}
Table~\ref{tab:sources} summarizes the sources and what live measurement
revealed about each. Three findings deserve emphasis because they
contradict reasonable expectations. First, the ENTSOG transparency
platform publishes confirmed daily flows at $\sim$09:20 CET the next
morning --- ten hours \emph{before} the official LNG platform's 19:30
publication of the same gas day, and its hourly series trails real time by
only two hours, so an intraday nowcast can lead the official print by half
a day using nothing exotic. Second, entire series can be silently absent:
the UK terminals stopped reporting to the European LNG platform before
2024 (974 consecutive days of null status), and one terminal's hourly
series consists entirely of rows whose values are null --- a naive
aggregation reads it as zero flow. Third, the platforms' own forecasts are
uninformative: archived nominations forecast realized send-out worse than
persistence at every terminal tested (Table~\ref{tab:eval}), with roughly
70\% of archived values being post-hoc renominations.

\section{How the official record revises itself}
\label{sec:revisions}
The United Kingdom publishes each day's send-out at three nominal
maturities, and --- unusually --- serves every historically published
version of every value, so its revision process is reconstructible
retroactively. Across 17{,}628 published versions (2024--2026): 27.8\% of
values are republished, but the median republication changes nothing ---
the D$+$2 commercial series is systematically re-issued about thirty days
later, almost always bit-identical. The informative structure is a heavy
tail: send-out corrections reach 662.7\,GWh and tank-stock corrections
340\,GWh (the latter arriving the next day). The ladder's final rung is
empty of information: the ``M$+$15'' items in fact publish at about
M$+$1 and never differ from the final D$+$2 value (maximum discrepancy
0.00\,GWh over 2{,}829 matched days). The next-morning physical figure is
within 0.02\,GWh of final at the 99th percentile --- so revision risk is a
rare-event phenomenon, not a diffuse noise floor.

The canonical illustration is Grain, gas day 2025-04-21: the physical D$+$1
series and the independent ENTSOG measurement both record $\sim$662\,GWh
--- near maximal send-out --- while the commercial series, having initially
agreed, was retracted to exactly zero a month later and finalized there.
The official record permanently disagrees with itself by a full cargo, and
that day's D$+$1 also published three days late: publication timing has
tails too. Any pipeline that silently trusts one official series inherits
such events, which is the concrete argument for heavy-tailed observation
models and multi-source triangulation (our cross-validation of the two
independent UK measurements agrees to 0.2--0.4\% of flow on ordinary
days).

On the European side no version history exists, so we archive the
platforms ourselves three times daily. A preliminary finding from the
young archive: the intraday ``estimated'' row for the running gas day
equals the previous day's confirmed value, digit for digit, at all four
terminals sampled --- i.e.\ the official intraday estimate appears to be a
carry-forward containing no information. We flag this as preliminary
pending a longer archive.

\section{Cargo arrivals from mass balance}
\label{sec:arrivals}
Tank inventories close the mass balance: $A_t = \Delta I_t + S_t$ up to
boil-off. Extracting arrival events this way over 2024--2026 yields
1{,}446 discharges. Their sizes constitute an independent test of the
whole measurement chain, because discharge energy is separately predicted
by vessel physics (class capacity $\times$ loaded fill $\times$ voyage
boil-off $\times$ retained heel $\times$ calorific content):

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
& median event (GWh) & 172k-class prediction & deviation \\
\midrule
Dunkerque & 1095 & 1096 & $-0.1\%$ \\
Gate & 1105 & 1096 & $+0.8\%$ \\
Zeebrugge & 1096 & 1096 & $0.0\%$ \\
Montoir & 1092 & 1096 & $-0.4\%$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent Four terminals' medians match independent physics to within one
percent. Attribution caveats are themselves informative: terminals with
multiple berths chain overlapping ships into multi-day clusters, and
small-tank terminals stage single cargoes across pauses (close pairs at
Dragon sum to Q-Flex energies, e.g.\ 1351.5 vs.\ the 1357 class value).

The arrival \emph{process} is emphatically non-Poisson. The empirical
hazard of an arrival at a one-day gap is exactly zero at every terminal
--- a berth-turnaround refractory period no memoryless model can produce
--- and rises with gap age at scheduled terminals (Gate reaches 0.56 by
day eight). The filter therefore drives arrivals with a Negative-Binomial
renewal hazard on observed days-since-last-cargo rather than a constant
rate. AIS draught inversion, the folkloric route to cargo size, is
strictly dominated where tank data exist: ballast compensation hides
roughly 60\% of the mass change, leaving $\pm$38\% errors against mass
balance's $\sim$1\%; draught earns its keep instead as a laden/ballast
classifier for ships in transit.

\section{A semi-Markov, Rao-Blackwellized nowcaster}
\label{sec:model}
\paragraph{States and regimes.} Let $S_t$ be send-out and $I_t$ tank
inventory (GWh) on gas day $t$. Send-out is a decision process with
metastable states: an \emph{idle} regime with $S_t=0$ exactly, and an
\emph{active} regime. Regime $r_t$ carries its age $a_t$, and switches
with the discrete hazard $\lambda_r(a)$ of a Negative-Binomial sojourn law
fitted to observed spell lengths --- the explicit-duration (semi-Markov)
structure. Sojourn laws are first-passage-time distributions of the
underlying decision process; their strongly non-geometric shape is why
constant-hazard Markov switching misfits long idle spells.

\paragraph{Conditionally linear-Gaussian dynamics.} Given the discrete
path (regimes, ages, innovation-component indicators $z_t$, latent
Student-$t$ scales $w_t$, and sampled arrival inputs $A_t$), the
continuous dynamics are linear-Gaussian:
\begin{align*}
S_t &= S_{t-1} + \eta_t, \qquad \eta_t \mid z_t, w_t \sim
\mathcal N(0, \sigma_{z_t}^2 w_t) \quad\text{(active, staying)},\\
S_t &\sim \mathcal N(\mu_r, \sigma_r^2) \quad\text{(active, entering)};
\qquad S_t = 0 \quad\text{(idle)},\\
I_t &= (1-b)\, I_{t-1} + A_t - S_t + \varepsilon_t,\qquad
\varepsilon_t \sim \mathcal N(0,\sigma_I^2).
\end{align*}
Each particle therefore carries only discrete variables plus Kalman
sufficient statistics $(m_t, P_t)$ for $(I_t, S_t)$; the continuous states
are integrated out exactly (the marginalized particle filter of
\cite{sgn2005}), and particle weights multiply exact Gaussian predictive
likelihoods. The idle regime \emph{is} the $S=0$ boundary, which
dissolves the truncation problem where it matters most. In practice 500
particles match a 4{,}000-particle bootstrap filter intraday at
fifteen times the speed, and the construction yields a byproduct no
bootstrap filter provides cleanly: a daily posterior probability that the
terminal is idle. One implementation subtlety deserves record: a posterior
\emph{view} at an intraday information set must apply the Kalman update to
every particle's mean, not merely reweight --- reweighting alone silently
destroys the intraday horizons.

\paragraph{The intraday observation channel.} Cumulative metered flow
through day-fraction $u$ satisfies $C(1)=S$, so the deviation
$d(u) = C(u)/S - u$ is bridge-like: $d(0)=d(1)=0$. The natural first model
is a Brownian bridge, $\mathrm{Var}[d(u)] \propto u(1-u)$. The data
reject it in an instructive direction: across 789--947 active days per
terminal, the empirical correlation $\mathrm{corr}(d(4\mathrm h), d(11\mathrm h))$
is 0.71--0.86 against the Brownian bridge's 0.49, and a fractional
Brownian bridge fits with $\hat H \approx 0.98$ at every terminal,
reducing correlation misfit by 81--87\% (Figure~\ref{fig:kernel}).
$H \to 1$ is the degenerate limit in which each day's deviation is a
single random ramp --- operationally, the morning reveals most of the
day. The filter uses the bridge form with terminal-specific mean profiles
$m(u)$ (Zeebrugge ramps $+2.4$\% by hour eleven) and robust per-hour
scales fitted on the training window only; the fitted kernel licenses
updating at arbitrary intraday times.

\begin{figure}[t]\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/fig_kernel.png}
\caption{Correlation of intraday deviations with the hour-4 value:
empirical structure (dots) against the Brownian bridge and a fractional

\label{fig:kernel}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Calibration with guarantees.} Model-based bands carry
interpretation; an adaptive conformal layer \cite{gibbs2021,gibbs2024}
carries the guarantee. For each stream we track the empirical quantile of
normalized errors by a Robbins--Monro recursion and report the adjusted
interval; long-run coverage at every nominal level then holds with no
assumptions on the data-generating process (the guarantee is marginal
over time, not conditional --- stated plainly). Walk-forward coverage
lands at 0.47--0.61 against a nominal 0.50 and 0.85--0.93 against 0.90
across all terminals, horizons, and eras.

\paragraph{Parameter learning.} Reference parameters are transparent
moment fits; particle Gibbs with ancestor sampling \cite{pgas2014} over
the semi-Markov trajectory provides full posteriors in seconds per
terminal. Its chief empirical lessons were about identifiability rather
than point estimates: blocks the data cannot see (idle sojourns at a
terminal that never idles) require tethering priors or the sampler
random-walks; mixture components label-drift on long histories without an
ordering constraint; and one restart-level posterior moved by a factor of
two against its moment fit. Parameter uncertainty is otherwise modest and
the filter's evaluation is insensitive to the refit.

Three structural readings. At the day-ahead horizon the filter ties
persistence \emph{everywhere} --- correctly, because the only public
day-ahead instrument (nominations) forecasts worse than persistence and
the fitted blend weight rejects it; genuine day-ahead information would
have to come from arrival pipelines and economics, which
Section~\ref{sec:econ} prices. Where an intraday feed exists, the evening
nowcast is 2--4$\times$ (post-crisis: 3--7$\times$) more accurate than
any daily baseline, half a day before the official print
(Figure~\ref{fig:bands}). And the uncertainty is honest: bands widen
exactly at regime breaks, and conformal coverage holds through the
crisis era.

\section{A physical stress index, pre-registered for winter}
\label{sec:stress}
Tanks plus burn rate define \emph{days of cover} --- how long current
send-out is physically sustainable without new cargoes. In-sample
(2024--2026), adding cover to the market's standard dial (storage
fullness) raises the AUC for predicting rare TTF jumps within 7--14 days
from 0.77--0.80 to 0.85, and for UK price spikes from 0.62 to 0.73. We
deliberately do not claim this: thresholds are in-sample, windows
overlap, only two winters exist, and two components show
seasonal-confounding signatures. The out-of-sample test is pre-registered
and imminent: Europe entered September 2026 with storage at 65\% against
a $\sim$90\% norm (Figure~\ref{fig:cover}), and the index will be scored
live through winter 2026--27 with the archive as timestamped evidence.

\section{Economic content: a comprehensive negative}
\label{sec:econ}
Does any of this move markets, and can it be traded? We answer in three
escalating tests, and the answer is a bounded, mechanism-consistent no at
the daily horizon.

\paragraph{Ordinary surprises are not priced.} Over 564 trading days, a
one-sigma ($\approx$109\,GWh) aggregate send-out surprise moves
front-month TTF by at most 0.16\% the same day, $|t|<1$ under
heteroskedasticity-robust inference --- noise against 3--5\% daily
volatility. Typical daily flow news is 1--3\% of regional demand and the
front month ignores it.

\paragraph{The backtest protocol.} Positions on front-month TTF at the
daily close, from signals verifiably published before it;
five-basis-point costs per unit turnover; continuous-contract roll
windows excluded; online z-scoring with no lookahead; Newey--West
$t$-statistics; every strategy variant counted against selection
(eight tried); and a holdout (2026) frozen until the strategy set is
final. Over 2018--2025 (1{,}642 tradable days, $\mathrm{se}(\mathrm{SR})
\approx 0.39$):

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{lccccc}
\toprule
strategy & SR (net) & $t_{NW}$ & maxDD & active \\
\midrule
arrival drought & 0.82 & 2.16 & 95\% & 100\% \\
12-day momentum & 0.77 & 2.03 & 88\% & 100\% \\
$\Delta$cover (14d) & 0.19 & 0.53 & 116\% & 100\% \\
cover level & $-0.18$ & $-0.45$ & 184\% & 100\% \\
long-only & $-0.23$ & $-0.58$ & 306\% & 100\% \\
storage fullness & $-0.66$ & $-1.66$ & 363\% & 100\% \\
flow news (continuous) & $-0.69$ & $-1.80$ & 151\% & 41\% \\
supply-break events & $-0.81$ & $-2.06$ & 92\% & 4\% \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent The discipline earned its keep twice. First, the development
window's best strategy ($\Delta$cover, SR 1.50 on eighteen months)
collapsed to 0.19 on full history --- selection luck, caught before the
holdout. Second, the last live hope --- trading supply-break events
detected intraday --- grew from 18 to 65 trades when the evaluation was
extended into the crisis years and returned SR $-0.81$: not zero but
\emph{wrong-signed}, with a coherent mechanism. At the daily horizon,
causality runs from price to flows as much as the reverse: extreme prices
themselves curtail send-out (diversions, slot cancellations), so
supply-loss events cluster near price peaks, and buying them loses. The
best surviving row is statistically indistinguishable from the plain
momentum beside it over a window containing the 2021--22 rally, and
neither clears best-of-eight selection. The market's standard stress
dial, storage fullness, is itself a poor directional signal over 6.5
years. The holdout remains frozen; nothing qualifies to touch it.

\paragraph{Interpretation.} These results are jointly consistent with the
damped-arbitrage structure of the LNG market
\cite{arbitrage2014,realoption2020} and with daily closes efficiently
impounding public physical information. What the nowcaster demonstrably
supplies --- accuracy inside the day and calibrated uncertainty at
breaks --- is valuable precisely at horizons and in instruments (prompt,
within-day, volatility) that free daily data cannot test and retail
access cannot reach. We state the monetization honestly: this is a
measurement instrument, not a trading system.

\section{Limitations}
No free historical satellite AIS exists, so the ship-pipeline layer of
the stress index accrues forward-only and is unvalidated. The intraday
Estimated-row finding rests on a young archive. Zeebrugge's transshipment
traffic leaves residual miscalibration at its intraday horizons (raw
coverage 0.65 at H2 in the crisis era; conformal repairs to 0.85).
Interquartile bands run somewhat wide. The stress index's positive
in-sample margin awaits its pre-registered winter. History floors bind:
ENTSOG's LNG series begin 2021, the UK portal's in August 2021. And the
backtest speaks only to daily-frequency, front-month, long/short
directional trading --- the negative is broad but not universal.
